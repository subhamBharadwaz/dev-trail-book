## The idea

A project explanation should cover the problem, your contribution, architecture, a difficult decision, the outcome, and what you would improve. Be precise about personal ownership versus work done by teammates, clients, libraries, or AI assistance.

## Why it matters

Prepare six concise stories about ownership, a difficult bug, a deadline, a failure, learning, and client communication. Use factual scope and outcomes. If you did not measure an improvement, describe the observed result rather than inventing a percentage.

## A practical example

```js
const story = {
  problem: "What user or client needed",
  responsibility: "What I personally owned",
  decision: "The trade-off I made and why",
  action: "The concrete implementation or investigation",
  evidence: "What I actually measured or observed",
  reflection: "What I would improve next time",
};
```

## Common mistakes

Do not create fictional client results, users, revenue, or performance metrics to fill a template.

## Try it yourself

Record a two-minute explanation of each of two real projects. Remove vague claims and make one trade-off explicit.
